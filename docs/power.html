What the signal says — each with the numbers behind it, then what it means and why, for an infrastructure operator.

Power demand across datacenter grids is holding steady.
Content-weighted demand is running -0.1% YoY. The last 3 months averaged +2.2% vs +4.1% the prior 3 — a -1.9pp change in the growth rate.
Power, not chips, is the gating constraint on AI capacity — this is the arena a digital-infrastructure operator competes in. Accelerating demand means capacity is tightening and energized sites gain scarcity value.
Fastest-growing grid: Southern Company (Georgia / Southeast).
+11.0% YoY, inflection +0.7pp (last 3mo +1.1% vs prior +0.5%).
This is where load is climbing fastest — so where power headroom is scarcest and the value of an energized site or a live interconnection position is highest.
Cooling — watch PacifiCorp West (Oregon / Pacific NW).
Still -10.9% YoY but inflection -5.5pp (last 3mo -6.1% vs prior -0.6%).
In a hot datacenter grid, cooling demand growth often isn't weak demand — it's the ceiling: grid headroom or interconnection queues throttling what can connect. Worth watching as a sign power is becoming the hard limit there.
Where power is scarce, it gets priced.
Southern Company +11.0% vs PacifiCorp West -10.9% — a 22pp spread in demand growth across grids.
Demand isn't uniform — it concentrates where land, power and fiber line up. Whoever holds energized capacity or queue position in the hottest grids has pricing power; that scarcity is the asset digital-infra deals underwrite.

Method: daily electricity demand for each balancing authority from the EIA API (Hourly Grid Monitor, EIA-930), averaged to monthly, then year-over-year growth and its inflection — last three months' average YoY minus the prior three. Grids are weighted by an estimate of their datacenter intensity. Pairs with the read-through (the component/demand pulse from Taiwan): this is the power/capacity side of the same story.
This is total grid demand as a proxy for datacenter load, not a direct datacenter meter — it's confounded by weather and economic growth. Year-over-year comparison controls for normal seasonality, but an unusually hot or cold year still moves it; read the cross-grid differences and the inflection, not the absolute level. Weights are estimates.
